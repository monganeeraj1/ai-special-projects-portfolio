Flagship case

Leading an airline fuel-efficiency AI programme

I advised a major airline's leadership on turning its fuel-efficiency objectives into an AI programme — a prioritised use-case portfolio, a business case, and an implementation roadmap. The platform combines fuel prediction, anomaly detection and flight optimisation, with agentic orchestration and reinforcement learning in the engineering scope.

The investment question was specific: could the airline identify a material opportunity, connect it to an action inside an operator's control, deliver the recommendation while it was still actionable, and measure the incremental benefit? A flight that burns more than expected is a reason to investigate — not a saving. Everything else followed from taking that distinction seriously.

Where the technical argument happens

An explanation is not proof

Feature importance and Shapley attribution explain a fitted model. They do not establish that changing a variable will cause the predicted saving. Separating a likely driver from a feasible intervention — and from evidence of incremental impact — is the difference between an interesting analysis and a recommendation an operator can act on.

How I think about AI

Six questions I keep coming back to.

01

Outcome

What decision, behaviour or operating outcome are we actually trying to improve?

02

Data

What signals exist, and are they trustworthy and available at the point of decision?

03

Model

Is the right approach prediction, retrieval, generation, optimisation — or something simpler?

04

Evaluation

What does “good” mean before deployment, after deployment and across operating segments?

05

Workflow

How does the output change a real decision or action rather than simply produce another dashboard?

06

Governance

What needs to be monitored, challenged, overridden, approved or stopped?
