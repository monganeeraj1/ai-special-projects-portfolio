How I think about blockchain

The useful question is not “where can we use blockchain?” It is “where is shared trust the actual constraint?”

01

Multi-party process

Several organisations need to coordinate but do not fully trust one another.

→
02

Shared record

A common, tamper-resistant history could reduce reconciliation or disputes.

→
03

Identity / provenance

Value depends on proving origin, ownership, status or authenticity.

→
04

Business model

The technology matters only if the governance and economics work for every participant.

Why agriculture was interesting

Agricultural value chains combine many participants, provenance questions, asymmetric information and coordination problems. That makes them a useful environment for thinking about what distributed trust can—and cannot—solve.

Technology evidence

This was not a claim of blockchain coding expertise. It was emerging-tech strategy grounded in how the technology works.

Technology thesis

Understand distributed ledgers, immutability, decentralised trust and smart-contract logic well enough to challenge use cases.

Use-case design

Separate problems that need shared trust from problems better solved by conventional databases or process redesign.

Operating model

Ask who governs the network, who has write / read rights, who pays and how disputes are resolved.

Business value

Connect technical properties to a measurable reduction in friction, reconciliation, risk or information asymmetry.
