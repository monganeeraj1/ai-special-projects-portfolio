Technology integration · Telecom

Rogers–Shaw — integrating technology, operations and customer migration at transaction scale.

Established the Integration Management Office with 54 consultants and operations specialists and led wireless integration activities across operations, technology, vendors, customer migration and more than 1,200 retail locations.

Integration problem

The transaction only created value if two operating and technology environments could become one.

Backend technology

Wireless integration required coordinating changes across the backend systems and technology dependencies supporting day-to-day operations.

Retail-store technology

More than 1,200 locations meant integration also had to work at the frontline: store systems, operational readiness and local execution.

Customer migration

Technology sequencing had to stay aligned with how customers, services and operational processes would move through the integration.

Vendor coordination

External and internal technology dependencies had to be pulled into one integrated plan rather than managed as separate technical projects.

The management question was not “is the system ready?”

It was: are the dependent systems, stores, vendors, operating processes and customer-migration activities ready together? That required an integrated technology-and-operations view rather than separate status reports.

What this case demonstrates

My technology value is strongest at the integration layer.

Systems thinking

Understand how backend technology, frontline systems and customer processes combine into one operating outcome.

Scale

Turn central integration choices into coordinated execution across a large physical footprint.

Cross-functional control

Run technology as one part of an integrated business transformation rather than a separate IT programme.

Executive delivery

Use governance to surface the few dependencies and decisions that truly change readiness.
