Technical depth

The model is one component of a larger decision system.

Actual production metrics are confidential; this monitoring view is reconstructed to demonstrate the evaluation and model-governance approach.

Technical questions I expect the team to answer — and how the prototype answers them

Target: What are we predicting, and is it measurable without leakage? An explicit feature contract; a test fails if ground truth ever enters it.

Validation: Does performance hold over time and by segment? Temporal split, baselines, a known noise floor, metrics by route family, fleet and station.

Uncertainty: When should the system abstain? Quantile models, conformally calibrated on held-out data; wide intervals and out-of-range inputs go to review, not into the ranking.

Causality: Which signals are actually levers? Counterfactual against benchmark operations, split by exact Shapley values, scored against the simulator's truth — including its bias.

Explainability: Can an operator see why? Each queued flight names its dominant levers and the kg attributed to each.

Drift: What changes performance? Feature, prediction and residual monitors with a season-matched reference; two injected scenarios with different signatures.

Model

Gradient boosting on a temporal split, reported as lift over mean and ridge baselines against the simulator's noise floor — it closes 84% of the achievable gap. p10/p90 quantile models, calibrated from 71% to 80% coverage with a split-conformal margin.

Attribution

Controllable excess = f(x) − f(x at benchmark). Exact Shapley over five levers; credits sum to the total to 1e-6. Correlation with truth 0.97; the conservative bias (−3 pp) is measured and explained rather than hidden.

Decision layer

Abstain → filter → rank on controllable kg → explain. On the same review capacity it reaches 98% of the oracle's capture with 100% actionable precision; ranking on the total prediction wastes 58% of operator effort on excess nobody can recover.

Monitoring & risk

A measurement-definition change barely moves feature PSI but shows up in residuals; a real operational shift does the opposite. Leakage, drift, automation bias, causal misreading and value misattribution each map to a control in the model card.

Value realization

A technically successful model can still be a failed AI investment.

Actual client economics are confidential; the value view reconstructs the investment logic.

Value = eligible opportunity × identification × actionability × adoption × realization × unit economics − run cost.

The important management question is not only whether the model predicts well. It is whether the organization can act on the signal and whether that action creates incremental, measurable value.

That is why benefits measurement, Finance validation and adoption belong inside the AI program — not after it.

What this case demonstrates

The work sits between strategy, AI and operating execution.

Strategy

Frame the opportunity, choose where to invest, define the value thesis and make explicit scale / stop decisions.

AI literacy

Engage credibly on target design, features, model class, validation, explainability, uncertainty, drift and causal limitations.

Transformation

Coordinate operations, data, technology, governance, vendors, change, Finance and executive stakeholders around one delivery plan.

Implementation

Turn model output into a real operating workflow with decision rights, adoption, monitoring, feedback and measurable benefits.
