TAQA Distribution — bringing structure to a multi-vendor technology transformation.

Led the cross-functional PMO spanning Abu Dhabi and Al Ain, focused on modernising customer systems, redesigning business processes and delivering enterprise-wide change across business, IT, field operations, vendors and corporate functions.

ScopeAbu Dhabi + Al Ain
TransformationBusiness + IT
Vendor ecosystemCore42 · TCS · Coforge · Oracle
Client situation

The challenge was not one system. It was a transformation spread across systems, processes, functions and vendors.

Scattered systems & processes

Customer-system modernisation and process redesign were moving across multiple parts of the organisation, with interdependencies that were difficult to see in one place.

Many functions in the change

Customer operations, IT, field services, Finance, Change Management, billing, support, revenue, regulatory, network operations and the CEO Office all had different delivery needs.

Multi-vendor delivery

Core42, TCS, Coforge and Oracle sat inside the transformation ecosystem, creating a need to integrate vendor plans with internal business and technology dependencies.

Integration ownership was fragmented

There was no single integration SME with a complete end-to-end view. The PMO therefore had to build enough integration understanding to connect system change, process redesign and delivery sequencing.

How I brought structure to the chaos

The PMO became the integration layer for the transformation.

Before

Fragmented delivery picture

Separate plans, inconsistent reporting, unclear dependencies, vendor-specific views and business decisions that could not easily be traced to system impacts.

→
After

One integrated control model

Common workstream architecture, milestone taxonomy, dependency map, decision log, cross-functional governance and a single management view of delivery.

Technology understanding

To run the programme well, the PMO had to understand the technology—not just collect status from IT.

Customer systems

Understand where modernisation changed customer-facing workflows, billing, support and service operations.

Enterprise / backend systems

Track technology dependencies that sat behind business-process redesign and cross-functional delivery.

Integration points

Build a working view of how systems, vendors and business processes connected when no single SME held the full integration picture.

Change into operations

Translate technology milestones into process, role, training, governance and operating-readiness implications.

What this case demonstrates

Technology transformation is often an integration problem disguised as a project-management problem.

Transformation control

Integrated business + IT PMO

One management system across technology, process, people, vendors and executive decisions.

Technology fluency

Enough technical depth to connect dependencies

Not acting as the system architect, but understanding the landscape well enough to challenge sequencing, ownership and integration assumptions.

Digital PMO

Dashboards and automation

Used digital tracking to make delivery information faster, more consistent and decision-oriented.

Cross-functional leadership

Business, technology and vendors in one room

Created a common operating rhythm across teams with different incentives, vocabularies and delivery constraints.
