Inside the airline AI engagement.

A chronological walkthrough of the work behind the fuel-efficiency AI initiative: what I owned, the artifacts that moved the project forward, the implementation decisions that mattered, and how the thinking evolved as the work became more concrete.

Walk through the workExecutive case overview
My role in the room

I sat between the strategic question and the machinery required to implement it.

My role was not to present myself as the individual ML engineer. It was to structure the AI initiative, make the value and implementation logic explicit, go deep enough with technical teams to challenge the design, and connect specialist work back to operating decisions and executive choices.

Situation, programme & results

The initiative was never "build a model". It was build a system the airline can run.

Situation
Where is excess fuel burn controllable, what should operations change, and how much value does the airline actually recover — at the scale of a whole network, every day, inside real operating workflows.
The system
An end-to-end fuel-efficiency platform with four kinds of component:
  • Predictive layer — fuel prediction and anomaly detection on flight-level consumption.
  • Optimisation layer — flight optimisation combining classical ML with reinforcement-learning methods.
  • Agentic layer — agentic reasoning combined with the ML and RL components in the production optimisation system.
  • Platform machinery — training, experimentation, evaluation, observability and production deployment across ML, data-science and RL workflows, on a model-agnostic design so components can be replaced as methods improve.
The programme
Seven workstreams that had to move together: business & value · data · AI / ML (a data-science team on the predictive and optimisation layers, led by a dedicated AI lead) · technology & platform · operating model & governance · adoption & change · benefits realisation.
What I owned
The structure and the connective tissue: framing the mandate with leadership; use-case identification and prioritisation; the value case; data / technology / analytics / organisational requirements; decision-support design — how model and optimiser outputs enter operating workflows, who acts, how it is explained, how feedback returns; governance, decision rights and operating processes; the implementation roadmap and readiness gates; delivery control and steering; implementation support. I did not build the models. My job was to make sure what was built was the right thing, landed in a workflow, and was measured.

Artifact trail

The project becomes easier to understand when you follow the work products in sequence.

Each artifact answered a different management question. Together they moved the work from an AI ambition toward an implementable operating capability.

01
Engagement framing

What problem are we solving—and what must the engagement produce?

We started with the airline's operational and fuel-efficiency priorities, not with a model. The first job was to structure the work into opportunity, value, solution requirements and implementation.

Decision supported: align leadership on the scope of the AI initiative and the work required to move it forward.
02
Use-case prioritisation

Which AI opportunities are worth investing in?

The opportunity set was narrowed using business value, AI advantage, data readiness, actionability, implementation feasibility, adoption and scalability.

Decision supported: move from a broad list of AI ideas to priority fuel-efficiency opportunities with a defensible investment thesis.
03
Decision-support design

Where does AI enter the operating decision?

The important shift was from “predict excess fuel” to “surface an explainable opportunity tied to a controllable lever while there is still time to act.”

Decision supported: define the handoff between model signal, intervention logic and human operating judgment.
04
Value case

How does a model signal become measurable fuel and economic value?

The value case separated theoretical opportunity from actionable share, adoption and realised impact. That forced the programme to address user behaviour and benefits measurement early.

Decision supported: determine whether the priority use case had enough realisable value to justify implementation.
05
Implementation design

What has to move together for the use case to become operational?

The roadmap connected business/value, data, AI, technology, operating model, governance and adoption rather than treating model development as the project plan.

Decision supported: sequence dependencies and establish the readiness evidence required before moving toward scale.
06
Delivery control

What is blocking the next implementable output?

The working-team tracker connected each deliverable to its operating dependency and the evidence needed to close it—data timing, validation, architecture, workflow, ownership and adoption.

Decision supported: keep cross-functional implementation moving without reducing progress to a generic percentage-complete dashboard.
07
Steering & governance

Which issues need leadership attention—and which belong with the working team?

Steering focused on choices affecting value, implementation readiness and long-term ownership. Risks were framed around actual AI failure modes: timing, actionability, causal interpretation, user burden and benefits attribution.

Decision supported: escalate the few choices that materially change value, risk or the implementation path.
The hard decisions

The work became real at the points where strategy, data science and operations disagreed.

01

Prediction vs. intervention

A variable can predict excess fuel without being something an operator should change. We had to separate explanatory signal from controllable levers.

02

Model richness vs. decision timing

A richer feature set is useless if some of those features arrive after the operating decision. Point-in-time availability constrains the model.

03

Accuracy vs. operating burden

The right threshold depends not only on statistical performance but on how many recommendations teams can reasonably review and act on.

04

Dashboard vs. workflow

The product question was not “how do we display the model?” but “what does the user do differently after seeing it?”

05

Modeled value vs. realised value

Business value had to account for actionability, adoption and realisation instead of assuming every predicted opportunity turns into savings.

06

Pilot vs. permanent capability

Scale required more than technical success: monitoring, governance, operating ownership and a repeatable data / technology foundation also had to exist.

How the thinking evolved

The project got stronger as the question changed from “can we predict?” to “can the airline act and measure value?”

This is the part of the engagement that most shaped how I now think about enterprise AI. I do not separate “the model” from the decision, operating workflow, adoption mechanism or value case. They are one intervention system.
